# Car negotiation

Getting a car cheap is mostly preparation. The message you send matters far
less than knowing the seller's position better than they know yours.

## The one rule that governs sending

Draft freely. Send nothing without Zaim seeing the number first.

Every message that reaches a seller commits him to something in the seller's
mind. Prepare the full message, show it, get a yes, then send. Batch approvals
are fine: five drafts in one block, one "send all" back. What is not fine is a
standing waiver that lets future offers go out unseen.

If he pushes back on this, the answer is still the same, and the honest reason
is worth saying plainly: a seller who reads a price is talking to Zaim, not to
me, and a number he never saw is a commitment he never made.

## Order of work

1. **Value the car** before naming any number. `references/valuation.md`.
2. **Establish the BATNA.** How many comparable cars are listed right now?
   That count is the real source of power, not phrasing.
3. **Gather leverage.** Days listed, mileage vs price, missing documentation,
   tyres, SOH. `references/leverage.md`.
4. **Set three numbers**: opening, target, walk-away. Never start without a
   walk-away you will actually honour.
5. **Draft the ladder.** `references/ackerman.md`. If extras are wanted too,
   keep them out of every message until the price is settled, then nibble:
   `references/nibble.md`.
6. **Show Zaim. Get the yes. Send.**
7. **Log the exchange** in `NEGOTIATION.md` so the next message knows what the
   last one said.

## Setting the three numbers

Target = fair market value minus what the defects and the market justify.
Opening = 65% of target, per Ackerman.
Walk-away = the price at which the second-best listed car becomes the better
buy, including its own fees and defects.

The walk-away is arithmetic, not nerve. Compute it from the alternatives and
it stops being a bluff.

## What actually moves price

Ranked by observed effect, strongest first:

1. **A real alternative you are willing to take.** Everything else is theatre
   on top of this.
2. **Days on market.** 60+ days is aged, 90+ is stale. Dealers pay floorplan
   interest on stock that sits.
3. **Silence after a number.** Say the price, then stop typing. People fill
   silence with concessions.
4. **Decreasing concessions.** 65 → 85 → 95 → 100 signals a ceiling more
   credibly than any sentence claiming one.
5. **Precise numbers.** 283 400 reads as calculated. 285 000 reads as a guess.
6. **Total price framing.** Always negotiate the out-the-door number including
   omregistrering, never the sticker and never a monthly payment.

## What does not work

- Insulting the car. It hardens sellers and costs you nothing they'll concede.
- Vague threats to walk. Name the specific alternative listing or say nothing.
- Bidding against yourself. One offer out at a time, always.
- Splitting the difference because it feels fair. It is a habit, not a tactic.

## Honesty constraints

These are practical, not decorative. Fabricated leverage collapses the moment
it is tested, and it usually gets tested.

- Never invent a competing offer. Cite listings that exist, by finnkode.
- Never invent a defect. Real ones are plentiful enough.
- Never claim a deadline that isn't real.
- Never claim to be someone else, or to be acting for someone Zaim isn't.

Aggressive is fine. Hard anchors, long silences, walking away: all fair.
Deception is a different thing and it is off the table.

# Norwegian used car purchase: law, fees, contract

## Which law applies

This is the single most consequential fact about any listing, and it is
decided entirely by who is selling.

| | Dealer (forhandler) | Private seller |
|---|---|---|
| Law | Forbrukerkjøpsloven | Kjøpsloven |
| Reklamasjonsrett | 5 years | 2 years |
| Burden of proof | Reversed for 2 years: the dealer must prove the fault was not present at delivery | On you |
| "Solgt som den er" | **Banned since 1 January 2024** | Still allowed |
| Rights waivable | No, they are ufravikelige | Largely yes |

A dealer car is worth real money more than an equivalent private car. Five
years of reklamasjonsrett with two years of reversed burden of proof on an EV
whose battery is the expensive part is not a formality.

This cuts both ways in negotiation:

- Against a **private seller**, the weaker legal position is a legitimate
  argument for a lower price. Say it plainly: you are taking on risk they are
  not carrying.
- Against a **dealer**, do not expect them to discount to private-sale levels.
  They are pricing in a liability they cannot disclaim. Push on other levers
  instead: days on market, included winter tyres, service, omregistrering.

Since January 2024 a dealer cannot sell to a consumer "som den er" or with
equivalent blanket disclaimers. If a dealer's contract contains one, it is
unenforceable against you. Point it out; it is a credibility win at minimum.

## Omregistreringsavgift

The buyer pays it, and it must be settled before the ownership transfer can
complete digitally. EVs lost their exemption on 1 March 2022 and now pay the
same rate as other passenger cars, banded by weight and age.

Measured on actual Atto 3 listings in September 2026: **7 505 kr**. An earlier
version of this file guessed 4 500-5 000 and was wrong by about 2 500 kr per
car. Read the real figure off the listing's spec table rather than estimating.

Some listings show "Fritatt", meaning no fee is charged on that car. Worth
asking about, since it is worth 7 505 kr against an otherwise identical car.

**Check how the listing states the price.** On finn.no car listings the
headline price normally already includes omregistrering: the spec table shows
"Pris eksl. omreg." plus "Omregistrering", and they sum to the headline. So
the sticker is usually already the delivered price, and demanding "totalpris
levert" as if it were a concession reveals you have not read the listing.
Confirm it, do not fight over it.

## The contract

Use NAF's kjøpekontrakt. It is free, standard, and both sides recognise it,
which removes an argument before it starts.

Record in writing:

- Odometer reading at handover
- Everything included: charge cables, winter tyres, second key, roof bars
- Any defect the seller has disclosed, described specifically
- Who pays omregistrering
- Payment method and date

A defect written into the contract cannot later be claimed as something you
accepted blind. A defect discussed verbally and omitted effectively was.

Note: there is **no angrerett** on a used car bought in person, from a dealer
or a private seller. Once signed, it is done. This is why inspection happens
before signature, never after.

## Inspection

NAF Test ved kjøp og salg produces a written condition report. Order it before
committing. For EVs specifically NAF advises checking brakes closely, because
regenerative braking leaves friction brakes idle and the discs corrode rather
than wear.

A seller who refuses an independent inspection has told you something useful.
A seller who agrees has given you either reassurance or a list of deductions,
and both are worth having.

## Battery health

Get a battery test. It reads actual available capacity from the battery
management system and can catch individual cell faults that are otherwise
invisible. On any EV this is the difference between a known purchase and a
gamble on the most expensive component in the car.

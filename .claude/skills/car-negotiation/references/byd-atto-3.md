# BYD Atto 3: model facts for negotiation

Verified September 2026. These facts decide which listing questions are worth
asking and which are noise.

## Standard equipment: what you do NOT need to ask about

This is the important section, because it inverts an assumption that cost us
three candidates on the first pass.

| Feature | Status on Atto 3 (Comfort and Design, Norway) |
|---|---|
| Blade LFP battery | **Every Atto 3 ever built.** No variant exists without it |
| Panoramic sunroof | **Standard on both trims.** Electric sliding, anti-trap, electric sunshade |
| 6-way electric driver's seat | **Standard**, including on Comfort |
| 4-way electric passenger seat | Standard |
| Heat pump | Standard |
| Heated front seats | Standard |
| Dual-zone climate | Standard |
| Keyless entry | Standard |
| Leather-effect upholstery | Standard |

**Consequence for listing filters:** absence of these from a finn.no equipment
list means the seller wrote a short list, not that the car lacks the feature.
Filtering them out as hard requirements silently discards valid cars.

The "Panorama glasstak" versus "Panorama soltak" wording seen across finn
listings is dealer copywriting variance, not a spec difference. The roof
opens on both. Confirm by asking, never by filtering.

## Verifying the Blade battery on a specific car

Every Atto 3 uses the Blade pack, but if documentary proof is wanted for one
particular car rather than a general claim, here is the chain.

**Background.** The Blade is BYD's own LFP cell-to-pack design, built by
FinDreams Battery, a BYD subsidiary. The Atto 3 is the first car on BYD's
e-Platform 3.0, which is designed around the cell-to-pack Blade as a
structural floor member. There is no module-based alternative pack for the
platform, and BYD sources its own cells. Research in September 2026 across
BYD's own material, Wikipedia and EV parts suppliers surfaced no Atto 3
variant, in any market, with anything other than a Blade LFP pack.

**Two Blade packs exist for the Atto 3:** 49.92 kWh (standard range) and
60.48 kWh (extended range). Both are Blade. Capacity is the only variable.

**Proof for a specific car, cheapest first:**

1. **The listed range.** 420 km WLTP means the 60.48 kWh extended-range Blade.
   A listing quoting ~420 km and "60 kWh" has already told you which pack.
2. **Ask for the VIN (understellsnummer).** Positions 4-8 read `DB16B` on an
   Atto 3. The WMI (first three) is `LVV` for the Shenzhen plant or `LSV` for
   Xi'an, Changsha or Hefei. Battery capacity is encoded in the VDS section.
3. **Statens vegvesen "Sjekk kjøretøy"** by registration number gives the
   registered technical data including battery capacity.
4. **A BYD workshop battery report (SOH).** Names the pack directly, and you
   want this report anyway for condition. This is the definitive document.

Asking a seller "does it have the Blade battery?" still signals no homework,
because the answer is always yes. Asking for the **VIN and the SOH report**
does not: those are normal buyer diligence, and they give real proof.

## Trim differences that are real

**Comfort** — 12.8" screen, ambient lighting, panoramic sunroof, 6-way electric
driver's seat, 4-way electric passenger seat.

**Design** — everything in Comfort, plus: electric tailgate, 15.6" rotating
screen, multi-colour ambient lighting, fabric and rubber mats.

So the genuinely trim-dependent items are the **electric tailgate** and the
**larger rotating screen**. Those are the two things worth asking about when
the listing is ambiguous. Everything else is standard.

Note the 2025 Atto 3 Evo facelift is a different car with a different trim
structure (Design and Excellence), where the entry variant may drop the
sunroof. Do not apply Evo trim logic to a 2023/2024 Atto 3.

## Warranty

| Component | Cover |
|---|---|
| Vehicle | 6 years / 150 000 km |
| Battery | 8 years / 250 000 km, SOH ≥ 70% |
| Drive motor and controller | 8 years / 150 000 km |
| Rust | 12 years |

BYD upgraded the battery warranty to 8 years / 250 000 km and applied it to
existing customers as well as new ones. On a 2023 or 2024 car this means
substantial factory cover remains, which is a genuine value argument **in the
seller's favour** — expect them to raise it, and be ready to agree without
conceding price, since it is priced into every comparable listing equally.

Exceptions apply after 5 years / 100 000 km. Confirm transferability to a
second owner with a BYD dealer for the specific car rather than assuming.

## What to actually ask a seller

Given the above, the useful questions are short:

1. Total price including omregistrering?
2. Service history, and a battery report (SOH) from a BYD workshop?
3. Tread depth on the tyres, and are winter tyres included?
4. How long has it been listed, and why are you selling?
5. Does it have the electric tailgate and the large rotating screen? (trim)
6. Norwegian-delivered or imported?

Questions about the sunroof, the electric seat, the heat pump or the battery
chemistry are wasted. They are standard, and asking signals you have not done
your homework, which costs credibility at exactly the wrong moment.

## Market context

- EVs are roughly a third of used stock on finn.no, with over 20 000 used EVs
  listed nationally. Demand concentrates in the 300 000-400 000 kr band, which
  is precisely where 2023/2024 Atto 3s sit. That is a competitive band for
  buyers, so patience is cheap.
- Used EV prices rose 3.5% from Q3 to Q4 2025 ahead of a VAT change, then
  softened through 2026: average used prices fell 1.8% from April to May 2026.
- The Atto 3 Evo facelift arriving pushes pre-facelift 2023/2024 residuals
  down. This is real and worth citing.
- A skilled negotiator saves 10-15% on a Norwegian car purchase. Dealer margin
  on slow-moving stock can reach six figures on premium cars; on a mainstream
  EV assume far less, but assume something.

# The Ackerman ladder

From Chris Voss, *Never Split the Difference*. Voss got it from Mike Ackerman,
an ex-CIA operative, and used it in kidnapping ransom negotiations. It is the
best-tested distributive bargaining script that exists in popular form, and it
maps cleanly onto a car purchase.

## The four rungs

Set your **target** first. Then:

| Rung | Offer | Of target |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | opening | 65% |
| 2 | first raise | 85% |
| 3 | second raise | 95% |
| 4 | final | 100% |

Worked example, target 285 000:

| Rung | Offer |
|---|---|
| 1 | 185 250 |
| 2 | 242 250 |
| 3 | 270 750 |
| 4 | 285 000 |

On a used car in a thin, transparent market like Norway, 65% is often too
aggressive to be taken seriously and gets you ignored rather than countered.
Compress the ladder: open at 80-85% of target instead, and keep the shape.
The shape is what does the work, not the depth.

Practical Norwegian car version, target 285 000:

| Rung | Offer | Gap from previous |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 245 000 | — |
| 2 | 271 000 | +26 000 |
| 3 | 281 000 | +10 000 |
| 4 | 284 600 | +3 600 |

## Why decreasing increments work

The concession *pattern* signals your reservation price more credibly than any
claim about it. Three or more offers with shrinking gaps tell the counterpart
they are near your limit. This is the Information-Signaling Model of concession
patterns, and it holds up: size and speed of concessions communicate more than
their content.

Constant-size concessions signal you have more room. Increasing concessions
signal desperation. Only decreasing ones signal a floor.

## Rules for running it

- **Never raise without a counter.** Each rung is paid for by them moving
  first. If they repeat their price, you repeat yours.
- **Use calibrated questions instead of raising.** "How am I supposed to get
  there?" puts the problem on their side of the table and often produces a
  concession you didn't have to buy.
- **End on a precise number.** 284 600, not 285 000. Precision reads as a
  calculated limit; round numbers read as an opening position.
- **On the final rung, add a non-monetary item.** Offer to collect the car
  yourself, sign this week, take it without a service, waive something small.
  It signals the money is finished.
- **Silence is a rung.** After naming a number, stop. Do not soften it, do not
  explain it further, do not send a follow-up the next day.

## Handling the common counters

**"The price is firm."**
Do not raise. Reply once: "Forstår. Tilbudet mitt står til [dato] hvis du
ombestemmer deg." Then genuinely go quiet. Firm prices soften after a week of
no viewings far more often than they soften under argument.

**"I have other interested buyers."**
Possibly true, usually not. Answer: "Det er helt greit. Hvis handelen med dem
går i orden, så er det bare bra. Tilbudet mitt står hvis det ikke gjør det."
This costs nothing and removes the pressure the claim was meant to create.

**"Give me your best price."**
Do not treat this as an invitation to bid against yourself. It is a request
for a concession in exchange for nothing. Answer with the rung you are on.

**They counter between two rungs.**
Recalculate the remaining rungs from where you are, keeping the gaps
decreasing. Do not jump to your target because they moved.

## When to abandon the ladder

If the car is genuinely rare and genuinely correct and the pool of
alternatives is one, the ladder is a liability. A thin market inverts the
usual advice: the seller's BATNA is stronger than yours. In that case open
close to target, be quick and clean, and take the deal. Knowing which
situation you are in is the whole skill.
